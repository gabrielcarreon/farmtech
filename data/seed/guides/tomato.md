## Climate Requirements
Tomato rainfall tolerance in Central Luzon is 400 to 600 millimetres across the growing period. The documented temperature requirements are 18 to 29 degrees Celsius. Cooler nights inside that band help fruit set, while long stretches above the upper bound raise flower drop. These requirements are the same thresholds stored in the crop table for this sample dataset. October through February is the usual planting window in Nueva Ecija because the season is drier. Planting into the wet core of the year increases disease pressure even when the seedbed itself looks fine.

## Water Requirements
Keep the root zone moist and avoid standing water. Furrow or drip irrigation is easier to control than overhead watering once fruit begins to color. A week of heavy rain can crack fruit and stain foliage even if the seasonal rainfall total is still inside the 400 to 600 millimetre band. Drain plots before planting. Do not treat a wet planting week as proof that the rest of the ninety-day crop will stay inside tolerance.

## Planting
Sample guidance uses a ninety-day determinate crop. Transplant sturdy seedlings rather than scattering seed into a crusted field. Space plants so air can move, because dense canopies hold leaf wetness after rain. Stake or trellis before the first fruit load. The planning horizon of a farm analysis should cover the whole period through harvest, not only the transplant day. If the horizon is shorter than the variety duration, the later reproductive weeks are simply outside the scenario.

## Harvest
Fruit is picked over several weeks once color breaks. Wet fruit bruises and should not be sacked immediately. This sample guide does not set a selling price. Use the market dataset for pesos per kilogram, and treat any single trading-post quote as one observation inside a range. Yield in the bundled sample sits roughly between 12000 and 22000 kilograms per hectare before weather adjustment. That band is a dataset field, not a promise for a specific parcel.
