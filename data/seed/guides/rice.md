## Climate Requirements
Lowland rice in this sample prefers 22 to 34 degrees Celsius. Rainfall requirements for the crop are 900 to 1800 millimetres over the growing period, or an irrigation supply that replaces a short rainfall total. Central Luzon palay is commonly established from June to August, with another window in November and December where irrigation is reliable. Temperature inside the band does not cancel a storm week during flowering. Note any extreme rainfall day separately from the seasonal total.

## Water Requirements
The paddy should hold water through tillering and be drained with intent before harvest. A burst of deep flooding can lodge plants even when the monthly climate normal looks typical. Drought stress at flowering reduces filled grains. If the forecast covers only the first part of a 120-day crop, later water stress has to be judged from climate normals, and the result should say so. That uncertainty belongs in the data-confidence score, not in a hidden adjustment of the price.

## Planting
The sample variety runs about 120 days, inside a 110 to 140 day crop range. Level the field so irrigation depth is even. Use certified seed where it is available, and keep a record of the lot. This guide does not invent a farm-gate price. Price rows in the market table are the only figures the profit scenarios are allowed to use. Historical yield observations in the sample are about 3500 to 5500 kilograms of palay per hectare depending on the year and province.

## Harvest
Cut when most grains are hard and the plot has been drained. Wet grain that sits in sacks heats up and loses quality. Record the date and the unit (kilograms per hectare) so a later import does not mix tons with kilograms. Storms in the last two weeks are a weather risk even after a favorable vegetative score. The analysis should show that risk beside the profit range.
